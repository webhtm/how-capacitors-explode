How Capacitors Explode

How do capacitors explode?

Let's first see how electrolytic capacitors (the most common ones) work

There are 3 elements

  • The outer hard shell
  • The two conductive plates
  • The dielectric (insulating the two plates to prevent short circuit)

    • The two plates hold electrical charges, while the dielectric prevents them from touching each other (causing a short circuit) and the electrolyte serves as a negative plate

    Because the electric charges attract each other, the dielectric prevents that but keeps the current in place, the charges wait for a short circuit (being free and touching each other)

    Now, let's actually see how they explode

    When the capacitor is attempted to charge with a higher voltage than the rated voltage, the dielectric breaks because the charges are too powerful, then it creates a low resistance (almost like a short circuit) and the electrolyte boils because of the heat (high current flowing through the broken dielectric) which is why it generates a lot of heat, and if the capacitor is very small or doesn't have a vent (scored lines on the top of the capacitor), the electrolyte boils and creates gas, and the body opens and creates a mess, vented capacitors will put out smoke/electrolyte or both

    How to avoid them

    As you may already know, it is annoying when they explode, because they not only stop working but create a mess, if one or more has already exploded in your electronic(s), you will have to replace them, it is recommended to replace them with a higher voltage rating, but a same microfarad rating (uF) which is the unit of how much charge it can hold in one go

    Also, choose a reliable brand like Rubycon, Nichicon, Panasonic, Teapo, Chemi-Con or KEMET if possible, and avoid brands like ChongX and Chengx, these are low-quality brands, if you want to use a mid-range capcitor brand like SAMXON, Elite, Cheng or LTec, it isn't really a bad choice.
